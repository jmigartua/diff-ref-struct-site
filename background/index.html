The programs that inspire

The programs that inspire

Requirements R04–R06. The long list of Rietveld programs, how the programs to study were chosen, and what will be recorded for each.

The long list

Nine candidates. The brief names FullProf and GSAS; the others are the programs most often met in the powder-diffraction literature, free and commercial. Five were shortlisted on the evidence below (log L008); the study was then limited to three, FullProf, GSAS-II and Jana2020 (L009), and read in the way set in L012: FullProf as the baseline inventory, GSAS-II and Jana2020 for what FullProf lacks. The facts on each card are the commonly known ones and are checked against the program's own documentation when it is studied.

Choosing the five

“Most used” is measured, not assumed. Four kinds of evidence were sought on 2026-10-08:

  • Citations. The citation count of each program's reference paper(s), per database (Google Scholar, Web of Science, Semantic Scholar, Crossref).
  • Facilities. What the neutron and synchrotron powder beamlines name or ship (ILL, ISIS, PSI, APS, ESRF, Diamond, ALBA).
  • Laboratory instruments. The analysis packages of the main diffractometer makers (Bruker, Malvern Panalytical, Rigaku).
  • Surveys. Published user surveys or bibliometric counts of the programs named in Rietveld papers. None was found.

The five (L008): FullProf, GSAS-II, TOPAS, Jana2020 and MAUD. Studied (L009, L015): FullProf and GSAS-II; TOPAS and MAUD set aside in L009, Jana2020 in L015. Excluded: HighScore Plus, RIETAN-FP, Profex/BGMN and SmartLab Studio II (PDXL).

The rule. Programs are ranked by the citations of their own reference papers. Facility and vendor evidence decides close cases. A program whose count could not be checked, or cannot be separated from another program's, is not shortlisted on this evidence. FullProf, GSAS-II and TOPAS lead on citations and each has a facility behind it; TOPAS is also Bruker's package. Jana2020 is fourth: its counts mix single-crystal and powder use. MAUD is fifth, the closest call: RIETAN-FP's count is higher but is shared with VESTA, and HighScore Plus has no citation count that could be checked.

The limits. The session's network policy blocked the citation databases' APIs (Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar) and the facility pages, so every number was read from a web-search result that quotes the page, not from the page itself. The snapshot dates are unknown, and the databases count differently: FullProf's 1993 paper has 20,552 citations on Google Scholar, 11,926 on Web of Science and 2,486 on Semantic Scholar. ISIS and ESRF named no program on their own pages in what was found; only user papers name one there. Where nothing was found, the table says not checked. The choice should be re-run from the databases directly before it is relied on beyond phase 0.

How the programs are read

Since L012: FullProf is the baseline. Its capabilities for crystal-structure refinement from powder data are listed one per row, each with what it does, where it is set in the .pcr file, and its sources. GSAS-II is then read only for what FullProf lacks within that scope, in physics and in workflow (projects, sequential and parametric refinement, constraints and restraints, scripting, GUI). Each gap says what FullProf has instead and cites both sides.

  • In scope. Rietveld refinement of crystal structures (atoms, occupancies, isotropic and anisotropic displacements, cell); Le Bail and Pawley; peak shapes and instrument description for laboratory X-rays, synchrotron, CW and TOF neutrons; background; size and strain broadening; preferred orientation, absorption, zero and displacement corrections; several phases per pattern and quantitative phase analysis; constraints, restraints, rigid bodies.
  • Later. Magnetic structures, modulated and superspace structures, stacking faults, indexing, structure solution, 2D image integration, PDF and total scattering: one line each, saying which program has it and where.
  • Out. Single crystal, 3D electron diffraction.
On this page

    What this is

    The working site of one project: to build a Mac app for Rietveld refinement of powder diffraction data, after a study of the programs the community uses for it. It holds the brief, that study, the design, a dated log of every step, the release notes and the manuals of the core and the app.
