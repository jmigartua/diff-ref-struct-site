Where FullProf cuts a peak: symmetric Wdt × the width without X·tanθ, except with a parametric resolution file

FullProf was run on modified copies of the refined Yb2ZrWO8 run, one parameter at a time, and each isolated window measured in its .prf (scripts, results and method in manuals/fullprof/runs/window_experiments/):

  • With a tabulated resolution file, the half-window is Wdt × the TCH width computed without the phase's X·tanθ. It stays the same from X = 0.1 to 1.5 while the peak width triples, and holds from Wdt = 4 to 40 (with X = 0.645: 0.0750·Wdt against 0.0753 at 72.9°, 0.1504 against 0.1510 at 139.9°). With X ≠ 0 the low-angle edge reaches one step further.
  • The Kα2 line and the Finger–Cox–Jephcoat asymmetry leave the rule as it is; with the asymmetry FullProf's marker moves, not the window.
  • With YVO3's parametric resolution file (Res = 1, Xins = 0.0402) the window is no longer symmetric. Its low-angle side is Wdt × that width (1.008 to 1.05 of it from 140° to 30°), its high-angle side shorter, from 0.92 of it at 29.8° to 0.79 at 139.9°. The core's symmetric window cannot reproduce that: the likely cause of YVO3's differences. The rule for the high-angle side is still to be fitted.

Also made: a preferred-orientation reference, Yb2ZrWO8-PO-March (March–Dollase about [1 1 0], G1 = 0.85), since no tutorial run uses preferred orientation. The exponential function gave none: this FullProf build reads its G1 (0.30, −0.30), lists 0.0000 and calculates the pattern without preferred orientation.

Phase 2, step 3: isotropic size and strain, the corrections checked on the runs, four runs of tutorial 6 in the profile test

Size and strain (P24). New module microstructure and drs mic: per reflection, what FullProf writes in its .mic file. The sources are:

  • the sample's widths of eq. 19 of the microstructure paper (manuals/fullprof/docs/Microstructural_effects.pdf, p. 4), added to the resolution file's;
  • the split into size (√I_G/cos \(\theta\), Y/cos \(\theta\)) and strain (√U tan \(\theta\), X tan \(\theta\)) that the .mic header states;
  • the TCH (H, η) (eqs. 9–10, p. 2) and the integral breadth of the pseudo-Voigt (eq. 7);
  • apparent size D = 1/β* (Scherrer, eq. 14) and maximum strain e = ½β*d (eq. 17).

CrysFML08's microstructure routines (deps-pycrysfml commit 2258e77, Pow_MicroStructure.f90) cover only the anisotropic models. Found on the runs:

  • β* is taken with λ1 for the λ2 reflections too (strains larger by λ2/λ1, 61.0327 against 60.8810 in YVO3).
  • A negative U enters as √|U|.
  • Where the resolution file's H_G\(^{2}\) is negative (NdNi-HTO above 150°), FullProf prints HGi and betaG as NaN and Beta_TCH as 0.
  • FullProf prints the apparent sizes and strains of the values after its cycle, and the widths and breadths of those it read. YVO3's cycle moves U from 0 to −0.0914 and X from 0.645 to 0.821; its printed strain 60.8810 is that of the shifted values, where the values read give 44.2415.

LorSiz and the size and strain models are anisotropic: version 2 (P25), refused with a message.

Widths at the Bragg angle. FullProf evaluates the widths at the Bragg angle, without Zero and the displacement terms.

Phase 2, steps 1 and 2: the .pcr and data readers, and the calculated pattern as FullProf computes it

The core reads FullProf's inputs and calculates the powder pattern from them (core 0.6.0). New modules:

  • pcr: single-pattern .pcr files (COMM format). It reads the job, the flags by the names of their comment lines, wavelengths, background, excluded regions, the refined globals, and phases with atoms (Jbt 0, 2, 4, 6, with betas and symmetry modes). It also reads profile, asymmetry, preferred orientation and absorption, and resolution files. Codewords are kept as written. All 151 tutorial .pcr files are read, and 214 of the 282 files of manuals/fullprof/ in all. The others are multi-pattern (12), time-of-flight (8), Nsc ≠ 0 (5), and magnetic or superspace formats.
  • data: Ins 0, 1, 3, 5, 6, 8 and 10, ported from the Fortran the manual prints (pp. 133–137), with Fortran's blank-ignoring fixed fields kept. Ins 12 (GSAS) follows GSAS-II's G2pwd_fxye.py (commit c13e730). \(\sigma\) and variable steps are kept.
  • profile: Gaussian, Lorentzian, pseudo-Voigt and TCH (manual eqs. 3.8–3.17, pp. 47–48), and Bérar–Baldinozzi (eq. 3.29, p. 54). Finger–Cox–Jephcoat is ported from CrysFML's Prof_Val (CFML_PowderProfiles_Finger.f90, commit 8ed8670) with its Gauss–Legendre tables (fcj_table, generated by make_fcj_table.py).
  • powder: the model built from a .pcr (eqs. 3.1–3.2, p. 41), with:
  • positions with Zero, Sycos and Sysin (pp. 56–57);
  • Caglioti widths with the resolution file's terms added;
  • the overall B (p. 58), preferred orientation (eqs. 3.31–3.32) and absorption (eqs. 3.33–3.34);
  • backgrounds: points, polynomial (eq. 3.4), Chebyshev (Nba = −5) and Fourier (eq. 3.4′);
  • several phases with their scales, and excluded regions;
  • agreement factors, and Poisson simulation (no external crate: a SplitMix64 generator, Knuth's method below a mean of 30 and Hörmann's PTRS above).
  • drs calc <file.pcr> [--data f] [--out f] writes 2\(\theta\), Ycal, Backg (and Yobs, \(\sigma\)). drs simulate writes an XYDATA file with Poisson noise.

What the core computes:

  • F(h) = Σ_j Occ_j f_j(s) exp(−B_j \(\mathrm{s}^{2}\)) Σ_g T_jg exp(2πi h·(R_g x_j + t_g)), with s = sinθ/λ. The inner sum runs over all the operators of the group, centring and inversion included. T_jg is the anisotropic factor of the image, with betas R β Rᵀ.

The manual (p. 102) defines it as "chemical occupancy × site multiplicity (can be normalised to the multiplicity of the general position)", so a full site has Occ = m/M, and the sum over all M operators then counts each position once. The language of the core is left open on purpose: Rust (shared with the Tauri side, fast, one binary), Python with compiled kernels (the GSAS-II route and the difes habit) and a Fortran or C++ kernel are the options, and the choice is a design decision taken after the study, with the reasons logged. The long list of programs holds nine candidates; the five to study are chosen with evidence, not from memory.

The work is to be done in Claude, and a Claude Code session on the web clones one repository. So the project is a single private repository, jmigartua/diff-ref-struct, whose three top folders play the parts of the three difes repositories: core/ (the refinement engine, as difes), app/ (the Tauri Mac app, as difes-shell) and ecosystem/ (data, tools, site and log, as ecosystem_site). The separation of core and app is kept by rule, not by repository: the core never imports the app.

L0022026-10-08finding

How the difes ecosystem is built, the pattern to follow

The difes project lives in three repositories side by side. difes is the physics core in Python: no GUI imports, every feature with a test and a command-line path, a README with conventions, a CHANGELOG generated from the site. difes-shell is a thin Tauri 2 window that starts the core as a local server and draws what it returns; no physics in the shell. ecosystem_site is a Quarto website (theme none, the family design kit) whose navigation, log, release notes and inventory are generated by tools/build.py from data/*.yml on every render, with a plain LOG.md next to it, a manual in two parts (using the program, theoretical basis), a test ledger, an algorithm registry and a download page.

The brief

Josu asks for a macOS app, built with Tauri and behaving as a Mac app, for Rietveld refinement of crystal structures from powder diffraction (laboratory X-rays, synchrotron, neutrons), in the line of FullProf and GSAS. Before any code: an inventory of the five most used refinement programs, what they do and how, the features they share and those unique to each, so that the feature set is the most ambitious one. Asked for by name: the refinement core, a live cycle-by-cycle plot as in FullProf, graphics for the data and for the results, interactive background selection, the complete space-group tables, a GUI that writes the control files, and a text editor made for the control and output files. Everything logged and reported, on a website holding the process, the release notes, the findings, the background and the manuals of the core and the app, worked the way the difes project is. The brief is kept verbatim and organised into nineteen requirements (R01–R19) on the Brief page.
